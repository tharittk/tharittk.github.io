### Intro
To whoever curious enough to reach this post, welcome. 

I'm trying to write this post so that it is *useful* the future readers (i.e., those who're interested in the program).

 To get a sense where I'm coming from, during the application process, I did some self-reflection and come up with this thought: 

*" With System Programming I found myself reading the textbooks on computer system on my own - just like reading any other books. It is something I did not do while I was an undergrad. I'm not sure what this is but I think I'm onto something."*

That's how I knew I should apply for this program - and see how it goes.

These are patches I sent upstream. Some are applied, some are still under review. If you never contribute to the Linux kernel before and somehow feel like having your patches in public record is cool, I guess you are in the right place.

### Background & Preparation

It is enormously helpful to have a **Operating System** class. I think any standard undergraduate-level course in any college will do. In case you don't have one, there is a Youtube lectures from [University of Wisconsin](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLzBbfbHQmjyuqBFJ8KpDdcvnLNkvPXbS-). The instructor is the one who wrote the book [Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces](http://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~remzi/OSTEP/).

 After I was accepted, I read through the book [*Linux Kernel Programming by Kaiwan N Billimoria*](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/linux-kernel-programming/9781803232225/). Most likely, the beginner like us will not contribute to the "core" kernel (like memory management, scheduler, cpu arch etc.) Those do have high barrier of entry and require deep understanding of the system.
 
 But reading that book helps in order to just know where everything is and how your subsystem of choices interact with the core kernel. It was about 2 weeks reading time for me - I was not sweat every detail while reading it.

 Another thing to note is: Linux kernel is a moving target so trying to find "the book" that does the job is an impossible pursuit. I highly suggest that you get comfortable being uncomfortable: you have to learn-as-you-go and read-as-needed. It never works for me to read the kernel documentation top-to-bottom linearly. It only works when I, even vaguely, know what I'm looking for.
